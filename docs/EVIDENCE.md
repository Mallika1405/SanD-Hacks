# What the guidance is based on (and how strong it is)

Anchor is a demo, not a medical service. Its in-the-moment guidance is **directive**: short imperative steps, no questions, no waiting. It moves from one technique to the next by itself. She can interrupt ("slower", "something else", "stop"), but never has to. The one question it can ask is a safety question, and only when she mentions a symptom that could be an emergency.

| Design choice | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Longer exhale, double inhale ("cyclic sighing"), gentle, never forced | [Balban et al. 2023, Cell Reports Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947): remote RCT, exhale-focused breathing improved mood and lowered respiratory rate more than mindfulness | One RCT of daily practice, modest effect. Not tested on acute panic |
| "It rises, peaks, and eases. You don't have to fight it." Let sensations pass | Practical panic guidance, e.g. [Rethink](https://rethink.org/advice-and-information/about-our-advice-service/get-help-now/self-help-guides/how-do-i-manage-a-panic-attack); the acceptance principle from CBT for panic | General-audience guidance, not a clinical guideline |
| Name the feeling: "this is anxiety" | [Lieberman et al. 2007](https://sites.lifesci.ucla.edu/psych-sanlab/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Lieberman_2007_PsychScience.pdf): labeling emotions reduced amygdala response (fMRI, n=30) | Mechanism in one small lab study, not proven clinical benefit |
| Short concrete instructions, soft steady tone, grounding, no "calm down", no false reassurance | [SAMHSA Psychological First Aid guidance](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916143942/http://mentalhealth.samhsa.gov/Disasterrelief/pubs/manemotion.asp) and National Center for PTSD stabilization guidance | Expert consensus and training material, not trial data |
| Tense-and-release of muscle groups | Progressive muscle relaxation is a standard anxiety-management technique | Established, moderate |
| Seek help if much longer than ~20 minutes or with chest pain | Rethink / NHS-style panic advice | General guidance |
| Intrusive thoughts: no reassurance about the thought, let uncertainty stand, return to the task | ERP-informed principle that reassurance can reinforce OCD-type patterns | Principle, not treatment |

**Left out on purpose:** cold-water face immersion (the sources were blogs and the effect is not well established; unsafe for some heart conditions), and anything that promises to stop an attack.

**Safety overrides everything.** Self-harm, chest pain, fainting, breathing trouble or confusion stop the exercises and show 988 and 911. These are never treated as anxiety.

**Live research.** Bright Data also finds techniques on public health sites (NHS, NIMH, Mayo Clinic and others), Gemini turns them into short directive steps (questions are rejected), and they are woven in after the first reviewed blocks, labelled "not reviewed by a clinician". They are never used for intrusive thoughts.

All exercises are marked `needs human review`. A clinician should review them before any real use.
